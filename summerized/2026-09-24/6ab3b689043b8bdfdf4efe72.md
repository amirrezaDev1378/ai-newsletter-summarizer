Broadly applying crawler blocks or noindex tags would also harm the legitimate study guides and manuals hosted on the platform.

## Strategies & Tactics

### [Add target keywords in the first lines of LinkedIn posts (1 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.linkedin.com%2Fposts%2Fcarterwirig_this-is-the-craziest-seo-opportunity-ive-share-7507871339580583937-s8hd%2F%3Futm_source=tldrmarketing/1/010001a0ce00fccf-a1878ec8-ca49-48c9-8cd0-69a60cb92982-000000/iq-aOKqIznMvVg-DMs98BmdkuFRQGuqbS75LbFIeUEA=452)

Placing target keywords in the first one to two lines of LinkedIn posts capitalizes on how LinkedIn constructs post URL slugs. This can help build a public association between your brand name and target search terms, enabling Google to surface your LinkedIn content for relevant searches.

### [Your Content Isn't Bad, But Nobody Knows Why They Should Care (3 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fthemarketinglabuk.substack.com%2Fp%2Fyour-content-isnt-bad-but-nobody%3Futm_source=tldrmarketing/1/010001a0ce00fccf-a1878ec8-ca49-48c9-8cd0-69a60cb92982-000000/k-4mnQzJ7QLysvRoVlizAEKGyaesgYjn2PSBYJdNjKE=452)

If an audience cannot describe your brand in a single sentence, the issue likely stems from positioning rather than writing quality. Audiences tend to follow predictable, recognizable accounts rooted in specific content pillars (such as distinct industry trends or niche social strategies) rather than accounts that jump between random topics daily.

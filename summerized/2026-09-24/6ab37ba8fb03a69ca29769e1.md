## 1) 🚚 Doing migrations right

LDX3 opened with a great keynote by [Will Larson](https://substack.com/redirect/afb6a1d4-bcf4-4e7e-959a-436feae660b1?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios), who described the *delivery* journey of his team at Imprint: from manual gates everywhere, to CI/CD, to agentic workflows.

Will’s talk was extremely practical (we need more stories like this), and framed these steps as **migrations**. We know a lot about migrating code, and most of the same logic can be applied to processes and workflows.

Here are the parts of Will’s approach that stuck with me:

* **Understanding people’s problems** — there’s a reason why people do things the current way, even if it looks flawed. To build something better, you need to understand current problems deeply, which is hard!
* **Getting buy-in** — Will stressed how, even if there was clear top-down input about the direction, the implementation was a collaborative effort where people were involved at all stages. At some point, Will wanted to migrate from Jira to Linear, and started by involving the people who were the biggest Jira fans. He knew that if he could address their concerns, the rest of the team would be easier to migrate to.
* **Draw hard lines** — inclusion and collaboration matters, but once a direction is taken, resources need to go to supporting that direction. In picking AI coding agents, Will settled on making Claude Code the recommended coding tool: people who wanted to use something else could do it, but would not receive support from the platform team.
* **Sweat the details** — with any migration there is always a long tail of stuff to be fixed and edges to be smoothed until things truly work for everyone. Keep working on it even when it seems that “most of the work is done”.

### (1) [Nobel Prize Picks 2026 Laureates](https://substack.com/redirect/9ae2f69a-f987-454c-a004-402ab93b98de?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

![Nobel Prize Laureate](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!JprQ!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7a799d4a-1950-4070-a1a8-e6d3550295f4_1630x1630.jpeg)

This week, the Nobel Prize committee began handing out the most coveted prizes in science, starting with The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. 

Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel were awarded the prize for their “discoveries leading to optogenetics, which makes it possible to switch on, or off, the activity of individual nerve cells in a living brain.” Optogenetics is a wild field—by shining light at cells, you can turn them on and off, and make them do different things. 

Other winners announced so far include:
* [Francis Halzen](https://substack.com/redirect/7dc46d77-dc9f-4a72-b72b-6c07f3bbed1d?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) in Physics “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin”
* [Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai](https://substack.com/redirect/086b1a9e-07cf-4ee2-baa3-38b26e41c9ab?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) in Chemistry “for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis”
* [Anne Carson](https://substack.com/redirect/4af8211d-03f4-4c14-ad14-d31f1ba68dac?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) in Literature “for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature”

It’s always wonderful to take some time out of every year to celebrate the world’s geniuses and their great achievements to humanity.

### (2) [Atomic Machines Comes Out of Stealth with Matter Compiler](https://substack.com/redirect/f0a1837d-375b-4710-9494-2598c6a67c0b?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

Just in case you thought we might not be living in the future, we are living in the future. 

A startup called Atomic Machines came out of six years in stealth with a product called the **Matter Compiler**: “an AI-native manufacturing system that builds working micro-machines from code alone. No per-product tooling. No process development. Different code, different machine.”

If the name sounds familiar, it’s because a matter compiler is a long-held sci-fi dream. In Neal Stephenson’s [*The Diamond Age*](https://substack.com/redirect/40457899-bdf2-49df-8794-249b391884c8?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios), he calls the machines they use to make everything up to and including entire islands “matter compilers.” Founder & CEO Jeff Holden compares the Matter Compiler to the ribosome, which builds every protein in your body from code.

The first product AM is building with the Matter Compiler is the [PrimeSwitch PS-150](https://substack.com/redirect/29dfded0-e9fa-442b-96ca-1d17cc5b9a5e?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios), a 9.5 mm relay that carries 150 amps and opens in 50 microseconds (~1,000x faster than a contactor), for the 800-volt DC data centers that AI is pushing the industry towards. No existing chip fab can process its materials or assemble its moving parts, but AM says it can, and is beginning to ship to early customers now. 

Atomic Machines wants to go after a whole new swath of sci-fi products: [gears the size of a grain of sand, robots that work inside the body, cooling built into AI chips](https://substack.com/redirect/dc20f530-e96c-46f9-bce7-c707fe07bf6b?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios), and even devices that run [instant blood tests](https://substack.com/redirect/e002b053-8f3a-43ff-ad2a-02e951fb2faa?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios).

Holden closes his essay with a line that we might tattoo onto the Dose’s forehead:

> **“We are in the early innings of everything.”**

### (3) [Anduril Investing $3.7B Into Arsenal-2 Shipyard](https://substack.com/redirect/949a802e-6ab9-4a50-a258-8d54a5f03b2b?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

One of the biggest open questions in manufacturing is [*Why Can’t the U.S. Build Ships?*](https://substack.com/redirect/74d3eba0-7a9e-4078-ace6-312ff163b2a2?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

Anduril is trying to answer that question with a definitive “We can.” The company announced that it would invest $3.7 billion into Arsenal-2, a planned two-million-square-foot shipyard at Sparrows Point outside Baltimore. The facility will manufacture components for Virginia-class nuclear submarines and is expected to create more than 3,100 permanent jobs.

The plan is to start with components like torpedo tubes and eventually work up to larger submarine sections. As part of the deal, the Navy has awarded Anduril a contract worth up to $2.9 billion, with payments tied to demonstrated production outcomes. 

Anduril calls Arsenal-2 a “software-defined shipyard,” meaning it will use software to design how work moves through the factory, automate where possible, and keep detailed digital records down to individual welds. Production in Maryland is targeted for 2030, with an earlier start at a California facility planned for 2028.

### (4) [Privo Patch Helps Oral Cancer Patients Avoid Surgery](https://substack.com/redirect/932c6ae0-a98e-492c-b5a0-d60421a5313e?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

![PRV111 Investigational topical nanoengineered patch](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cD4c!,w_497,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F46b8f641-06fd-46d8-b5d8-106c9aa2bde0_497x500.jpeg)

Here at the Weekly Dose, we’ve long taken the brave and controversial stance that cancer is bad and should be eliminated. The challenge is usually targeting cancer cells and only them for destruction. 

On Monday, Privo reported results from a small Phase 2 study of PRV111 in patients with non-invasive oral cancer or high-grade precancerous lesions. Instead of getting surgery to remove the cancer from their mouths, patients applied a patch directly on the lesion to deliver chemotherapy.

After treatment, biopsies showed complete clearance in 21 of 22 patients. The remaining patient’s lesion improved to low-grade dysplasia. All 22 avoided their planned surgery, and no recurrences had been observed in the treated areas at a median follow-up of 14 months. 

The patch delivers cisplatin directly into the affected tissue while keeping systemic exposure elsewhere in the body negligible, resulting in no serious treatment-related adverse events.

### (5) [Biohub and Others Commit $1.8B to Data for Virtual Cell](https://substack.com/redirect/f9bddd97-23ba-49c0-bdb6-b8d299bd6a56?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan’s Biohub announced an expanded $1.8 billion collaboration to build the data needed for predictive models of cells. 

Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs, and Meta are collectively investing $300 million, joining Biohub’s existing commitment and contributions from the Department of Energy and NIH. 

The idea behind virtual cells is to understand cells well enough that scientists can ask what would happen if they changed something and get a useful answer from a computer. Virtual cells are a holy grail that should allow researchers to try thousands of possible interventions digitally at computer speed, accelerating towards real treatments more quickly.

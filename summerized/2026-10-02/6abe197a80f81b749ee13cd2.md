### 🥇 [If We Do Not Stop to Help Each Other, What Do We Become?](https://substack.com/redirect/0241c714-717f-46e6-8adf-5d81ccb4a6c4?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)
*3 min • by Jeff Atwood*

Jeff shares a great message from a developer who once got through a dangerous deployment because strangers on Stack Overflow chose to help him. An LLM could have provided the same (or a better?!) answer, but not the feeling that somebody cared. We need that reminder!

---

### 🥈 [Dishwasher Mode for AI Tasks](https://substack.com/redirect/d6f9d301-de2c-4551-bd58-49811aa4dd0e?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)
*1 min • by Clemens Adolphs*

A dishwasher is slower than washing plates by hand, but we don’t care, because it still frees up our time. Clemens uses the same frame for AI: give it small, annoying tasks off the critical path, then care more about reliable completion than raw speed. I liked this a lot.

---

### 🥉 [The Sensible Defaults Playbook](https://substack.com/redirect/d5592360-96d8-420a-b939-853d709411d7?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)
*2 min • by Thoughtworks*

Thoughtworks shares its collection of good defaults. Their approach is to have defaults remove recurring friction, but without becoming dogma. Start with practices that usually work, adapt them to the context, and review them as the environment changes.

### Core Message
The intersection of AI and product management highlights a fundamental shift: while AI excels at routine tasks and lowers the cost of building software, holding consumer attention requires distinct novelty, authenticity, and strong product fundamentals. Success is increasingly tied to effective distribution, intentional product scope, and treating software more like dynamic content.

---

### Valuable Takeaways

#### **News & Trends**
* [**The Coming Product Retention Crisis**](https://andrewchen.substack.com/p/adversarial-creativity-and-the-coming): As apps become cheaper to build via AI, they risk resembling disposable content. Long-term success will shift from traditional user retention to consistently producing fresh hits and building resilient brands.
* [**How to Give Away Free Product and Make Money Doing It**](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/how-to-give-away-free-product-and): Providing free AI access can be a highly efficient customer acquisition strategy if managed properly—treating free offerings as acquisition costs with defined payback periods, fraud controls, and eligibility rules.

#### **Opinions & Tutorials**
* [**7 Weak Product Management Signs**](https://dpereira.substack.com/p/7-weak-product-management-signs): AI can accelerate weak product management habits, making them harder to spot. Core fundamentals remain paramount: deeply understanding the problem, focusing on measurable outcomes, and owning the product direction.
* [**Software as Content**](https://adaptivesoftware.substack.com/p/software-as-content): AI is transforming software into remixable, highly shareable content, potentially positioning social feeds as the next major app stores.

#### **Resources & Tools**
* [**Product Strategy Starts With What You Will Not Build**](https://uxcrush.com/product-strategy): True product strategy is defined just as much by deliberate sacrifices and rejections as it is by the features you choose to pursue. 
* [**What I Learned From Building and Exiting a Startup**](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/what-i-learned-from-building-and-shutting-down-a-startup-kirsten-mann-strategic-advisor/): Positive demo feedback isn't a true validation of market demand. Founders must test for real user friction, follow authentic behavioral data, and treat willingness-to-pay and distribution with equal importance to the product itself.

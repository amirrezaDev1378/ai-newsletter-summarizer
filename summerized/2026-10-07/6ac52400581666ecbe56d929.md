# The state of the tech industry in 2026

*A look into what has changed, what’s still the same – and what’s broken – in the tech industry. Summary of the keynote at LDX3 New York by Gergely Orosz (Oct 6).*

---

I recently delivered the keynote at the [LDX3 engineering leadership conference](https://substack.com/redirect/7db8cfb0-21ee-496a-8219-9d12fbe10dac?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) in New York, attended by 2,000+ engineering leaders, CTOs, Director+ and Staff+ engineering folks, in which I attempted to create a snapshot of where the tech industry is at this exact point.

![In the middle of my keynote at LDX3](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wmBO!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F55ac4f75-983e-45c6-bde0-40307f3bcd03_2048x1366.jpeg)

It came after I had the chance to visit the AI labs OpenAI and Anthropic, meet innovative startups like Ramp, Uber and others, get new, unpublished data from GitHub, Factory AI and Linear. 

Before the conference, I spent weeks identifying and recording trends that didn’t exist a year ago – or were much more nascent back then – and also things that are currently broken, and others that remain unchanged. Today, we cover:

1. **Speed of change.** In the tech industry, change has never been this large-scale, this fast. Industry legend Martin Fowler confirms.
2. **What’s changed:** practically nobody writes code by hand anymore, working with 5-10 agents more common, the fading of the IDE, and 13 more changes from the last year.
3. **What’s broken:** assumptions about code output broke, code reviews became theatrical, quality and reliability is down, and more.
4. **What’s still the same:** teams are still important, as is planning; non-engineers are still not shipping code, and more.
5. **What comes next?** Cloud coding agents and harnesses, engineers to stop reading the code, companies building a new type of AI infra, and more are already starting, and should accelerate.

---

## 1. Speed of change

People in tech are more than used to change; seeing the internet go from zero to everywhere, the arrival of mobile communications and smartphones exploding in popularity, cloud computing rising from a niche concern, and the development of programming languages like Go and Rust, and frameworks like React (web) and Jetpack Compose (Android).

Despite this, the scale and pace of the impact AI is having at present is still without precedent. That’s how industry veteran Martin Fowler described it [at The Pragmatic Summit](https://substack.com/redirect/961a9b42-aedb-4ddc-b429-54ac52155bcc?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios):

> “**Nothing has hit with the magnitude of AI.** This is a whole size difference from anything that we’ve faced before.
> 
> On a smaller scale, we were very much involved in the growth of object-oriented languages. [object-oriented languages] scared a lot of people, but it didn’t scare us so much because we were part of it.
> 
> The internet had a huge impact upon us all. And, of course, we were spreading the challenge of agile software development. [Agile] had a very big impact on a lot of organizations because you could tell by how hard they resisted it.
> 
> But [for all those other changes] we kept talking about how important they were and how valuable they were and trying to persuade people of the importance of them. It may sound surprising, but even for the internet, there were people who wouldn’t think that was important!
> 
> **For AI there’s no argument about how important it is.** You cannot put blinkers on to deny the importance of this thing.”

![Martin Fowler (left) at The Pragmatic Summit](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sfe-!,w_1100,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4092a6d4-f212-4dbc-98fb-c7cf13d9630b_1555x868.jpeg)

My own take is similar; we are without doubt at the beginning of a massive technological transformation, and after it, software will be built differently from how we’ve been doing for decades, although some parts of development will remain the same. At the very least, the tooling and best practices will look different, and things are changing faster than ever.

## 2. What’s changed?

### #1 Nobody writes code by hand anymore
As the calendar enters Q4 of this year, we see this has solidified into a mega trend since the end of 2025 year when models greatly improved at coding. Related to this, in the very first article of this year we asked the question: [when AI writes almost all code, what happens to software engineering?](https://substack.com/redirect/dbaa8dc4-462a-4365-bfad-1af4db1ea6cb?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

Today, there are plenty of signs that most engineers have stopped writing code by hand, and it was also a sign of the times recently when the Ruby on Rails creator sparked a “death of coding by hand” debate. Personally, I’m excited by the new opportunity that AI creates, and it’s exciting to experience this revolution firsthand.

### #2 Working with several parallel agents is increasingly common
I like talking to engineers at AI labs because their working practices are often a few months ahead of the rest of the industry. Claude Code creator, Boris Cherny, revealed the new way he gets things done [on the podcast:](https://substack.com/redirect/6f9924d3-09f1-4bd5-a185-3b3e41da232c?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios)

> “**I have 5 terminal tabs.** Each one of them has a checkout of their repository. I’ll round robin and start Claude Code in each one. I also run 5-10 Claudes on Claude Web, in parallel with my local Claudes.”

Peter Mattis (Cockroach Labs cofounder) noted a similar approach:
> “Oftentimes, I’m doing things in parallel. **I find my cognitive overhead is about 5-10 agent sessions concurrently.** But sometimes those sessions will have many subagents doing things.”

Dima Zaytsev (Linear) told me:
> “It used to be a simple world: one mouse, one keyboard, one screen. You physically can’t work on more than one thing at once. Now, you no longer have that limitation.
> 
> **I end up having 5-10 (literally) worktrees locally that I rotate between.** Prompt one agent, and while it is working, move on to another to test output or review code.”

### #3 Rapid rise of agent-generated PRs
Fresh data that GitHub shared with me:

**Food for thought: there were more agent-authored PRs in August 2026 on GitHub than human-authored ones!** It’s reasonable to speculate that the number of agent-generated PRs will be permanently exceeding human-generated ones on the platform, going forward.

Agents are not just generating PRs, they’re also opening tickets. Data from Linear, which added [first-class MCP support](https://substack.com/redirect/2cf44325-af55-44e5-802c-3c5dd36effe5?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) for agentic creation of issues:

### #5 Agent skills usage is mainstream
Both engineers and non-engineers use agent skills, and lots of companies are building their own agent skills repositories for all staff to create, share, and evaluate. Data from [Factory AI](https://substack.com/redirect/69581802-17cb-4209-b859-9c640b38f386?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) shows 83% of users leverage skills, up 2.5x from February.

### #6 The fading IDE
Steve Yegge noted on a [podcast episode](https://substack.com/redirect/7c84aee6-715d-4a00-afaa-661fde2ea45f?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) that the IDE is effectively over for advanced AI users. Market data backs up this shift:
* **Antigravity 1.0** was the last IDE released on a VS Code fork (Nov 2025). By v2.0, it moved away from the IDE concept.
* **Codex** launched as a non-IDE in Feb 2026.
* **Cursor** [relaunched](https://substack.com/redirect/a5fbebdb-5aa4-4919-8600-3c518ae6c1d6?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) in April 2026, dropping the traditional IDE interface for an agentic layout.
* **JetBrains** is pivoting to agentic development environments like [JetBrains Air](https://substack.com/redirect/bdf91c95-ebac-4a62-b3db-ef32e691a593?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios).

### #7 Everyone is building their own harness/agent platform
Most mid-sized-and-above companies have built their own agent harnesses, including:
* Ramp (Inspect)
* Stripe (Minions), Uber (Minion), Block (Goose), Shopify (River)
* Google (Agent Smith), Meta (Devmate), Amazon (Kiro + Crew), Dropbox (Nova), Spotify (Honk)
* DoorDash (Flux), Grab (LLM-Kit), WorkOS (Horizon), Hubspot (Crucible)
* Monzo (Agent Chip), Sierra (Pinecone), Harvey (Spectre), Browserbase (bb)

### #8 Dev work often starts in Slack
At OpenAI, it’s “@Codex, implement this”; within Anthropic, it’s “@Claude build this”, while inside Linear, it’s “@Linear work on this”. More startups have their own Slack integration of their coding agent.

### #9 Agentic software factories being built everywhere
[OpenAI](https://substack.com/redirect/b3b3665e-b031-421a-9252-63723caeb196?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) and many other companies are building agentic software factories, adding AI functionality to existing systems like CI/CD, agentic code reviews, and deploy systems.

### #10 Migrations no longer take years
* **Anthropic**: migrating Bun from Zig to Rust [in 11 days](https://substack.com/redirect/f54dc702-b5aa-4fc0-842c-91ba6ca62314?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios) (vs 1.5 engineering years).
* **OpenAI**: API layer migrated from Python to Rust in [5 months](https://substack.com/redirect/f4757d33-5709-44be-8a56-af1e57901fa5?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios).
* **Airbnb**: 3,500 test files from Enzyme to React Testing Library took [6 weeks](https://substack.com/redirect/f0185ec9-c327-4bbc-9dd2-5fecbc59addd?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios).
* **Uber**: 600,000 JUnit 4 tests migrated to JUnit 5 took [4 months](https://substack.com/redirect/9c3a491b-5448-4594-9c69-aaef46f7c79c?j=eyJ1IjoiOTI4ejYxIn0.tSgkqY3JYQitX21MR6bNgb_LPnkIwNwTgbZDYVmbios).

### #11 AI costs a major engineering concern
Companies are cutting back on AI spend or moving to open models to save 50%+ on token costs through smart routing.

### #12 Projects get done with only one or two engineers
Katelyn Lesse (Anthropic) notes that individual projects rarely have more than two people because each engineer is already running several competing agents.

### Other changes
* **13. Engineering specializations are disappearing**: Lower demand for dedicated iOS, Android, and frontend roles; surge for generic software engineers and FDEs.
* **14. Teams are getting smaller**: Projects completed by fewer people.
* **15. Reduced junior recruitment**: Harder for graduates/interns to land entry-level roles.
* **16. Agentic infra is becoming its own discipline**: Infra teams morphing into agentic infra teams.

## 3. What’s broken?

### Assumptions about code quantity and frequency
Code quantity and commits are no longer growing linearly—they are growing exponentially.

### (Human) code reviews are dead
> “**Everyone is playing the theater of doing reviews**, but with the volume of changes that get thrown your way, I observed that people just find a path of least resistance: give up on reviews and just stamp everything with LGTM.”

### Agent-only code reviews trending up
PRs reviewed solely by AI agents are on the rise across platforms like Linear.

### Quality and reliability down
Software has increasingly become a brittle mess, with 98% uptime becoming the norm rather than the exception.

### Infra capacity shortage
Beyond GPUs, the market faces growing CPU shortages and reservations booked months in advance.

### A hit on personal focus and productivity
Constant context-switching between monitoring 5–10 active agents has increased the cognitive workload for individual engineers.

### Engineering leaders taking career breaks
CTOs, Heads of Engineering, and VPs are increasingly stepping down from management to return to hands-on building.

---

## 4. What’s still the same?

* **Teams are still important:** Two-pizza teams and structured ownership are still relevant, even when supercharged by AI.
* **Planning still happens:** Complex projects still require extensive upfront documentation and architecture planning.
* **Tests and validation are critical:** Writing tests for AI-generated code takes the same relative effort as before to ensure production trust.
* **Non-engineers are still not shipping prod code:** PMs and designers prototype and create bugfixes, but engineers remain responsible for production releases.
* **Old patterns work great with AI:** Concepts like "tracer bullets" (golden paths), unit tests, and design patterns help agents output better code.

---

## 5. What comes next?

* **Cloud coding agents + harnesses will dominate:** Developers will increasingly run agents in the cloud rather than locally.
* **Engineers will stop reading the code:** Much like Ops and QA long ago learned to ship code they didn't manually write line-by-line, developers will follow suit.
* **New internal infrastructure:** CI/CD pipelines, agentic observability, and deployment pipelines will be reinvented for agents.
* **A golden age of refactoring:** Tech debt will be cleared out rapidly via cheap, fast automated rewrites.
* **AI positivity in hiring:** Startups are actively screening for enthusiasm and fluency in AI tooling.
* **Domain knowledge increases in value:** As general coding intelligence becomes a commodity, wisdom, charisma, and deep business domain expertise become primary differentiators.

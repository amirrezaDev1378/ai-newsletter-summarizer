### [The Expensive-Loop Problem (22 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.lesswrong.com%2Fposts%2F2mStxuNDMdaWdyHu6%2Fthe-expensive-loop-problem%3Futm_source=tldrai/1/010001a1357cd128-eac7cc7f-b38a-4638-9aac-5035008a2c8c-000000/4Fq-aZcJbHiVMDDsL-cSfzx9R7pful2KITy4QZmCess=452)
Cheap-loop domains are domains where experiments are quick, deterministic, and cheap. Expensive-loop domains are restricted by real-world constraints or costs. Most high-value human labor is done in expensive-loop domains. Expensive-loop tasks require the ability to build theories, manage proxies, budget time and compute, and diagnose what went wrong when feedback is scarce, something models don't learn in cheap-loop training.

## 🧑‍💻 Engineering & Research

### [Can we change an AI model's architecture without retraining it from scratch? (14 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.amazon.science%2Fblog%2Fcan-we-change-an-ai-models-architecture-without-retraining-it-from-scratch%3Futm_source=tldrai/1/010001a1357cd128-eac7cc7f-b38a-4638-9aac-5035008a2c8c-000000/KSo-N5Ggv5kFAw06JkMJjB_htMW5BdbXJvvV03pBaqY=452)
Priming is a technique that involves using pretrained Transformer weights to build models with more efficient architectures at less than 1% of the original training cost and with throughputs up to twice as high. It allows researchers to explore the design space, the layer type, the hybrid ratio, and layer placement cheaply and systematically. Every variant starts from the same strong initialization and requires minimal training compared to pretraining. Priming is not tied to a specific Transformer family or model class, and extending it to other models requires no algorithmic changes.

### [When Do We Need On-Policy Distillation? Distilling on Offline Student Rollouts Is Often Better (1 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fresearch.nvidia.com%2Fpublication%2F2026-12_when-do-we-need-policy-distillation-distilling-offline-student-rollouts-often%3Futm_source=tldrai/1/010001a1357cd128-eac7cc7f-b38a-4638-9aac-5035008a2c8c-000000/81Q9AlbJR06j7vGqyPGYeS-ZHbFMq8lRqAZN_7I3H_0=452)
On-policy distillation has become increasingly popular for transferring teacher capabilities to student models. Semi-OPD, which distills from offline rollouts generated by the initial student, can often outperform OPD in both accuracy and training efficiency. The choice between OPD and Semi-OPD depends on the alignment between the initial teacher and student. OPD is beneficial only when the two are highly aligned with high overlap ratios.

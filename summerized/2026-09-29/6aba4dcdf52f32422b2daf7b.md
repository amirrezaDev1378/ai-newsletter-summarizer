## Strategies & Tactics

* **[How to Use Customer Language for Powerful Marketing Copy (11 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.wordstream.com%2Fblog%2Fcustomer-language-in-marketing%3Futm_source=tldrmarketing/1/010001a0e7bfdb3f-e9a31d9d-e4e9-4ea7-9990-1e963227dadf-000000/_Q44QGEFKvB6TptE7B4Kww1OD_iBwjkQAtzxmFG0bI8=452)**
  Gather customer phrasing from 15 to 20 recent reviews, support tickets, sales calls, Google Ads search terms, and AI chats. Tag recurring phrases to use in headlines, CTAs, ads, service pages, and FAQs. To capture AI visibility, ask prospects who found you via ChatGPT or Perplexity to share their conversations, including follow-ups and competitor comparisons. Start by changing one headline or CTA, review results after 30 days, and repeat quarterly.

* **[How long should your free trial be? (9 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fwww.revenuecat.com%2Fblog%2Fgrowth%2Ffree-trial-length%3Futm_source=tldrmarketing/1/010001a0e7bfdb3f-e9a31d9d-e4e9-4ea7-9990-1e963227dadf-000000/evFghqYjkdz6es41U_2AYelonZZUAQ_JMXIyxZiOKJo=452)**
  Evaluate trial lengths based on how quickly users experience value, their required return frequency, and the cost of free access. Test short trials against ranges like 5–9, 10–16, and 17–32 days per subscription plan, tracking conversions and first renewals separately. For monthly plans, testing 10–16 days often outperforms assuming longer is always better. AI apps must also account for per-user inference costs, while regional and category differences can dictate varying trial lengths.

* **[How X's ranking algorithm works (3 minute read)](https://tracking.tldrnewsletter.com/CL0/https:%2F%2Fjohnjwang.com%2Fpost%2F2026%2F09%2F25%2Fhow-xs-ranking-algorithm-works%3Futm_source=tldrmarketing/1/010001a0e7bfdb3f-e9a31d9d-e4e9-4ea7-9990-1e963227dadf-000000/2BrAEGzUoUuJo83AGsO1F_1JKk2qwfMIKkcFRypNID4=452)**

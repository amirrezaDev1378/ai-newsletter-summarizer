## 🧠 Deep Dives & Analysis

### [>10x More Efficient Pretraining](https://magic.dev/blog/pretraining)
Startup Magic has developed a pretraining recipe that is over 10 times more compute-efficient than leading open-weight models. They argue that efficient pretraining, agentic reinforcement learning, and long-context capabilities are key to building superhuman coding agents.

### [Inside the Megakernel Serving Engine for North Mini Code](https://cohere.com/blog/megakernels)
Cohere presents a serving system built around a decode megakernel that reaches 292 tokens per second—1.58x faster than vLLM. It supports continuous batching and paged attention with no loss of accuracy across batch sizes.

### [Pretraining Progress is Mostly Data](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/pretraining-progress-is-mostly-data)
Analysis shows that between 2019 and 2025, compute efficiency gains have come significantly more from data improvements (3.24x) than from model architecture changes. While small models see massive gains from data quality, these improvements may scale differently for larger models.

## 💻 Engineering & Research

### [Introducing Mercury 2.5](https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-2-5)
Mercury 2.5 is the largest diffusion language model ever trained. It performs comparably to frontier models like GPT-5.6 and Gemini 3.5, outputting 1,107 tokens per second with a 260K-token context window.

### [Hyper-π-bench: Evaluating Agents that Build Agents](https://sierra.ai/blog/hyper-t-bench-evaluating-agents-that-build-agents)
A new benchmark evaluates developer agents in sandboxed workspaces. Results show that while high-reasoning models like Claude Opus 5 pass 23.9% of tasks alone, they reach 82.2% success when paired with an engineer with deep context.

### [Google's AlphaGenome Maps 9 Billion Genetic Variants](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/)
Google DeepMind released the AlphaGenome Atlas, a 1-petabyte database that predicts the regulatory effects of all 9 billion possible single-nucleotide variants in the human genome.

Agent observability on the Claude Platform (3 minute read)

Users can now send OpenTelemetry traces from Claude Managed Agents, the Messages API, and Claude SDKs to any OTel tool, and follow each step their agents take. Claude traces follow the OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions, so data from Claude and other providers works in the same tools and dashboards. They work with any other OpenTelemetry tool that accepts a standard OTLP endpoint. The new feature can be used to debug agents, improve agents with production data, and track usage across an organization.
Analyzing technical documents with Deep Review (12 minute read)

Google's Deep Review is an agent AI system engineer designed to provide rigorous feedback and technical verification on complex documents. It was initially designed for computer science academic paper analysis, but its capabilities have since expanded to reviewing documents across many domains. Deep Review will initially support PDFs and plain text as input formats, but support for multi-file inputs will arrive soon. Google believes that tools like Deep Review will play a significant role across the peer review process in the coming years by empowering both authors and reviewers.
Google Harness² for Recursive Self Improvement (GitHub Repo)

Google Research's Harness² lets agents iteratively improve their prompts, tools, memory, and orchestration without changing model weights. It refines the harness for each task and then carries evidence-backed lessons forward to improve future tasks.

Routers Are a New AI Primitive (13 minute read)

Routers have emerged as a new primitive of the AI stack. They promise to handle the complexity of model management and help companies benefit from new models without revisiting model selection across every application themselves. Every router shares the same fundamental design, but they differ in how they decide. This post provides an independent comparison of current routers and looks at how today's routers compare with single models on quality and cost.
It Ain't AGI Until H100s Go for $30 an Hour (7 minute read)

Stratospheric GPU prices will be one of the easiest indicators of AGI to monitor and trade on. A 10x price jump in the price of AI compute will be the clearest sign that agents can finally outperform humans at most economically valuable work. The price would need to rise to an estimated $30 per H100-hour to reach this point. Supply will always be limited to the amount of available compute in the world, so something has to give.

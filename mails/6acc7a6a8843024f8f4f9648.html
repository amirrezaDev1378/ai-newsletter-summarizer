📚 Knowledge capture compounds engineering quality

In April, Rob Zuber and I wrapped up our software factory series with an article on compounding. We asked: how do teams actually get better over time, instead of just shipping more?

I believe knowledge capture is one of the few things in engineering that truly compounds.

Teams usually capture too little of it. They especially lose decisions, forgetting why systems work the way they do.

There is basically no excuse today for not getting this right, because capturing and processing information is much cheaper than before. Meeting notes, ADRs, product decisions, can all be captured, summarized, updated, and reused by agents.

So docs become an asset that improves the development loop, instead of a tax. And the more context the team captures, the more coherent decisions the AI can make on the next project.

This is especially important for building the right thing, as opposed to simply the next thing. A codebase becomes easier to steer when future work can see past decisions.

🤝 Good engineering is an exercise in human relationships

Two years ago I had Kent Beck on the podcast, and we talked about Extreme Programming and TDD. I rewatched that interview recently to figure out what has changed (and what has not) since then, and a part that stuck with me is when he said good engineering is an exercise in human relationships.

This is easy to miss because you may just treat XP as a bag of techniques: short cycles, tests, pairing, reviews, and so on. But all of these only work if people communicate and give each other feedback.

This ties in with Kent’s idea that Agile goes largely against traditional power structures. Waterfall represents a fatal attraction for many teams because it replaces grassroots communication with a plan that gets transmitted down through hierarchy.

If the human relationships are missing, you can install the practices and still not have the actual engineering.

6 min • by Francisco Trindade

Francisco takes on the question of whether we should still hire junior engineers, and his answer is a strong yes.

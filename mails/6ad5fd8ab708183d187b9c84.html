Telling AI to act as your customers makes its answer worse (7 minute read)

AI-generated customer personas can overestimate purchase intent and mislead product or campaign decisions. In one test, AI predicted that 52% of shoppers would buy a product when only 33% did. In another, AI personas were less accurate at predicting headline clicks than a model asked directly to choose which headlines readers would click.
Google's When to Buy Guide (8 minute read)

Google's 2026 holiday shopping guide uses historical pricing data to identify when discounts peak across retail categories. Black Friday weekend offers the best selection and savings for electronics, shoes, fragrances, and appliances. Late December brings deeper discounts on cosmetics, handbags, and kitchenware. Search data also reveals last-minute shopping patterns. “Next day shipping” searches peak December 22, while “same day pickup” peaks December 24.

How the AI boom might really end: 5 scenarios (7 minute read)

The AI boom may end not because demand disappears, but because profits shift away from major AI labs. Open-weight models are driving prices down while efficiency breakthroughs could reduce computing costs. AI companies risk failing to capture the value they create while chipmakers and infrastructure providers could still benefit from growing AI usage even if leading AI labs lose value.
What on earth is going on with AI pricing and packaging? (8 minute read)

AI companies are struggling to price products because usage costs vary widely and customers can't predict what credits or tokens will buy them. ChatGPT and Claude advertise vague usage limits, while Lovable sells credits without explaining what customers can build with them. Unlike traditional SaaS pricing based on seats or transactions, AI costs depend on the complexity of each task and the resources needed to complete it. Companies must make usage limits and costs easier to understand while managing unpredictable computing expenses and preventing abuse of free plans.
